How to spot the ISS from India

  • The ISS looks like a very bright, fast-moving star — the brightest object in the night sky after the Moon.
  • It rises in the west, crosses the sky in about 3–6 minutes, and sets in the east.
  • Best seen just after sunset or just before sunrise — the sky is dark but the station is still sunlit 400 km up.
  • It moves noticeably faster than an aeroplane and does not blink or flash.
  • No telescope needed — your eyes are enough.

Facts

  • Orbits Earth about 16 times a day — one orbit every ~93 minutes.
  • Crewed continuously since November 2000; typically 7 astronauts aboard.
  • Size of a football field — ~109 m across its solar arrays.
  • Visible from 95% of Earth's inhabited land — including all of India.
